04 Stakeholders & Challenges

Shared responsibility

A mandatory SSR standard only works if government, industry, and the workforce each carry a defined part of it.

01

Ministry of Minerals & Energy

Enforces rules and regulations regarding SSR installation, penalizes violations in accordance with the law, and determines which quarry types require an SSR unit.

02

Quarry companies

Mandated to install SSR units within their mining operations, and bear the cost of installation and regular maintenance to ensure accurate measurements and readings.

03

Ministry of Labour & workers

The final cornerstone: workers are ultimately granted a safer, more secure environment, free from the fear of imminent danger from falling rocks.

Based on the project's stakeholder analysis. See full sources.

Anticipated challenges

An innovation with its own limits

SSR is an intricate innovation, and it comes with challenges that should be anticipated rather than ignored.

False signals from moving vehicles

SSR can pick up false signals from moving vehicles, which can lead to reduced work hours as unnecessary evacuations are called to prioritize safety.

Dust and sand sensitivity

SSR is fundamentally sensitive to dust and sand accumulation, which can build up and interfere with signal transmission.

Source: Stickley & Reeves (2010), U.S. Patent No. 7,768,442. See full sources.

Kept in check

Mitigation through routine maintenance

These problems can be solved with essential, scheduled maintenance — a manageable operating cost against the risk it removes.

Daily & weekly checks

Daily visual inspections and weekly antenna cleanings keep dust and sand buildup from degrading signal quality.

Monthly & annual verification

Monthly signal verifications catch drift early; radars are recalibrated once a year to keep readings accurate.

Power kept online

Where units run on a generator, diesel levels must be maintained so monitoring never lapses.

Spares & longevity

Spares are guaranteed for 5 years, and some radar units have remained operational for more than 12 years.

Sources: report maintenance guidance and T. Van Zyl (personal communication, 22 July 2026). See full sources.
