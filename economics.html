05 Economics

The economics of prevention

The primary obstacle to widespread SSR adoption is its cost. Framed against the alternative, that cost looks very different.

One unit, fully installed
$300K–450K

Full-scale SSR systems represent a significant capital investment: around $300,000–$450,000 per unit, including installation, software, training, and ongoing support.

Source: T. Van Zyl, personal communication, 22 July 2026. See full sources.

Not simply an expense

A risk transfer

A single rockslide, like the one in Ibri, might not cost a company much in direct financial terms — but it cost 13 precious human lives. Framed like this, SSR isn't simply an expense; it's a risk transfer, exchanging an unmanaged hazard for a monitored, insured one.

Source: Yousuf (2022), Oman Observer. See full sources.

A maturing market

Costs are set to fall as adoption rises

As adoption increases and competition among manufacturers intensifies, the laws of supply and demand suggest unit costs are likely to fall — improving the feasibility of a mandatory SSR policy over time.

This supports the case for early adoption: as the global market grows from roughly $285M (2024) toward $661M (2033), unit prices should trend downward — making early adoption at a high-risk site like Ibri a reasonable, forward-looking first step rather than a premature one.
