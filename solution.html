02 The Solution

Radar that predicts disaster

Slope Stability Radar (SSR) continuously measures the movement and acceleration of rock material to detect developing instability, and triggers an alarm to warn workers before a slope failure occurs.

Source: Reeves et al. (2001). See full sources.

  1. 1

    Radar sends X-band wavelengths at the rock face.

  2. 2

    Waves bounce off rock and debris, and are intercepted by the radar.

  3. 3

    The system builds an interferogram, mapping millimetre-scale movement.

  4. 4

    Experts analyze interferograms to predict failure — the alarm triggers evacuation of equipment and personnel.

Process described in Reeves et al. (2001). See full sources.

Built for the site

Why Real Aperture Radar (RAR) fits Ibri

SSR units built on Real Aperture Radar (RAR) technology are tolerant of dust, vibration, and fog from ongoing quarry operations — conditions that are unavoidable at a working site like Ibri's. Where other sensing methods can be disrupted by an active quarry, RAR is designed to keep monitoring through it.

Tolerant of real quarry conditions
  • Dust and airborne particulate from blasting and haulage
  • Vibration from heavy equipment and ongoing operations
  • Fog and reduced-visibility conditions
